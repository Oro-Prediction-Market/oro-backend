# Oro Prediction Market Strategy

**Prepared for:** Oro team  
**Prepared by:** Gelay / ChatGPT strategy memo  
**Date:** 26 May 2026  
**Working thesis:** Build Oro as a simple yes/no prediction market on Telegram, supported by SEO-first content verticals that convert users into Oro markets across sports, politics, Bollywood, pop culture, crypto, esports, and creator communities.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Oro should not be positioned narrowly as a sports product, sportsbook, casino, fantasy-sports product, or generic crypto trading platform.

The cleanest strategic position is:

> **Oro is a simple yes/no prediction market on Telegram.**  
> Users pick Yes or No on real-world outcomes. Markets settle in USDT on TON. The UX is simple enough for mass users, while the market, settlement, compliance, and risk systems underneath remain serious.

The product should be global, not Bhutan-linked. User-facing language should avoid any Bhutan connection unless there is a separate regulatory or corporate reason to disclose it in legal documents.

The content strategy should not turn Oro into a magazine company. Instead:

> **Oro remains the product.**  
> **Cricket.oro.fun and Football.oro.fun are the first media + SEO + acquisition arms, but the long-term model also includes politics, Bollywood, pop culture, crypto, esports, and creator-led markets.**

The key strategic insight:

> **Build the media pages people search for. Use those pages to educate, acquire, and convert users into Oro’s Telegram prediction markets.**

World Cup 2026 is useful, but it should be treated as a beta event. IPL 2027 should be treated as the first major commercial target. After the cricket/football playbook works, Oro can expand into politics, Bollywood, pop culture, crypto, esports, and creator/community markets.


### 1.1 Internal Ownership Note

Oro is a **21 Tech product**. 21 Tech is registered in **Gelephu**.

For internal strategy, compliance, product planning, vendor discussions, and team execution, treat **21 Tech as the product owner/operator** unless this is later changed.

User-facing pages should still remain global and neutral. Do not make the public football or cricket pages about Bhutan or Gelephu unless there is a legal, licensing, trust, or disclosure reason to do so.

## 2. Core Strategic Decisions

### 2.1 Oro’s primary identity

Oro should be positioned as:

> **Simple yes/no prediction markets on Telegram.**

Suggested core line:

> **Pick Yes or No on real-world outcomes. Simple markets. USDT settlement. Built for Telegram.**

Alternative lines:

- **The simplest prediction market on Telegram.**
- **No charts. No order books. Just Yes or No.**
- **Predict real-world outcomes in USDT, directly inside Telegram.**
- **Polymarket logic, stripped down for normal users.**

### 2.2 What Oro is not

Oro should not sound like:

- a sportsbook
- a casino
- a lottery
- a fantasy-sports app
- a meme trading terminal
- a complex crypto exchange

Avoid these words in user-facing copy unless legal counsel explicitly approves them:

- bet
- wager
- gamble
- casino
- odds
- bookie
- jackpot
- guaranteed win
- risk-free profit

Preferred words:

- predict
- pick
- Yes/No
- market
- outcome
- settlement
- position
- probability
- maximum loss
- source of resolution
- challenge friends
- leaderboard

### 2.3 Product simplicity

The user should see only the simplest possible action:

```text
Will CSK win tonight?

[ YES ]   [ NO ]

Amount: 10 USDT
Maximum loss: 10 USDT
Settlement source: official match result
```

The complexity should be hidden:

- market pricing
- liquidity
- settlement logic
- fees
- resolution rules
- wallet checks
- KYT
- compliance
- fraud monitoring

### 2.4 Geography and target markets

Oro should not target the U.S. initially. The U.S. already has Kalshi and Polymarket-style products, and it is a complex regulatory market.

Oro’s real opportunity is in markets where:

1. Telegram usage is strong.
2. USDT usage is familiar.
3. Users are mobile-first.
4. Existing prediction markets feel too complex, too U.S.-centric, or too trader-focused.
5. Events, fandoms, social groups, and community identity are culturally powerful.

Priority regions to study:

- South Asia diaspora
- Southeast Asia
- Africa
- Latin America
- Middle East / Gulf expatriate communities

Each country must be assessed separately for legal and operational risk.

## 13. Prediction Market Design

### 13.1 Recommended economic structure

To preserve the prediction-market thesis, Oro should be designed as binary event contracts, not fixed-odds sportsbook bets.

Recommended structure:

- Each market has two outcomes: Yes and No.
- Winning side settles at 1 USDT per contract.
- Losing side settles at 0 USDT.
- User’s maximum loss is shown upfront.
- Markets are fully collateralized.
- Resolution rules are stated before entry.
- Source of resolution is stated before entry.
- Fees are transparent.

### 13.2 User experience

The user does not need charts, candles, order books, market depth, or complex trading language.

User sees:

```text
Will RCB make the playoffs?

YES price: 0.42 USDT
NO price: 0.58 USDT

Your amount: 10 USDT
Maximum loss: 10 USDT
Potential settlement: shown clearly
```

### 13.3 Trading out / cash-out

For MVP, Oro can keep it simple:

> Pick Yes/No → hold until settlement.

Later, Oro can add a simplified exit button:

```text
You bought YES at 0.42.
Current value: 0.61.

[ Hold ] [ Sell ]
```

No charts are required.

### 13.4 Resolution rules

Every market must answer:

- What exactly is being predicted?
- What source resolves it?
- What time does it close?
- What happens if the event is postponed?
- What happens if the match is abandoned?
- What happens if official results change later?
- What happens if data sources disagree?

This should be written before the market opens.

## 14. Compliance and Legal Guardrails

This section is not legal advice. It is a strategic risk checklist for counsel and product teams.

### 14.1 Do not assume “prediction market” solves all regulation

Calling Oro a prediction market does not automatically make it legal everywhere.

Different jurisdictions may treat binary event markets as:

- gambling
- betting
- derivatives
- event contracts
- financial products
- online money games
- prize competitions
- illegal wagering

The economic structure, marketing language, settlement asset, user location, and event type all matter.

### 14.2 India is high-risk for real-money launch

India is commercially attractive for cricket, but legally sensitive.

The Government of India’s PIB note on the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 says the Act imposes a complete ban on all forms of online money games, whether chance, skill, or mixed, and also prohibits advertising, promotion, facilitation, and payment processing linked to such games. [Source: PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?ModuleId=3&NoteId=158400&id=158400)

Therefore, for India:

- IPL content can be public.
- Free prediction games may be explored only after legal review.
- Real-money USDT markets should not be offered to India-resident users without a strong legal basis.
- Advertising real-money markets into India should be avoided unless counsel approves.

### 14.3 U.S. is not the initial target

The U.S. is not the intended launch market and should be blocked initially unless Oro obtains specific legal clearance.

### 14.4 Required controls

Oro should plan for:

- jurisdiction geofencing
- age verification
- KYC where required
- sanctions screening
- PEP/adverse media screening for higher-risk users
- wallet ownership verification
- KYT / wallet-risk scoring
- deposit limits
- loss limits
- self-exclusion
- cooling-off periods
- suspicious activity monitoring
- market manipulation monitoring
- bot detection
- audit logs
- customer support escalation
- dispute and resolution process

### 14.5 TrustPass opportunity

Oro should not build all compliance logic as one-off code.

TrustPass can become the shared compliance layer for:

- Oro
- Lucky Pem Global
- future USDT products
- BTN stablecoin use cases
- Telegram-native products

TrustPass should provide:

- KYC
- AML
- KYT
- wallet risk
- transaction monitoring
- sanctions / PEP screening
- case management
- audit trail
- product-level risk rules

## 15. FIFA, IPL, Athlete and IP Caution

### 15.1 FIFA / World Cup

FIFA says it protects brand assets including logos, words, titles, symbols, and other identifiers connected with the World Cup and FIFA events. [Source: FIFA Brand Protection](https://inside.fifa.com/tournament-organisation/brand-protection)

Therefore:

Do not use:

- FIFA logos
- World Cup trophy imagery
- official emblem
- mascot
- official typefaces or visual system
- “Official World Cup prediction game”
- “FIFA partner”
- any wording that implies endorsement

Safer wording:

- Oro Football
- Global Football 2026 Guide
- Football Prediction Guide
- World Football 2026
- 104-Match Guide

### 15.2 IPL / cricket IP

The same principle applies to IPL, BCCI, teams, logos, jerseys, and player images.

Until rights are cleared:

- avoid official logos
- avoid team crests
- avoid protected broadcast clips
- avoid official match footage
- avoid implying affiliation with IPL, BCCI, or franchises
- use editorial text and licensed or generic visuals

### 15.3 Athlete image rights

Do not use athlete images commercially unless:

- licensed
- embedded from official platforms in compliance with platform rules
- from properly licensed image providers
- used under clear editorial basis after legal review

## 19. Other Oro Verticals Beyond Cricket and Football

> **Oro is a simple yes/no prediction-market layer for any public outcome that people care about.**

The vertical expansion strategy should be disciplined. Each vertical needs clear outcomes, reliable resolution sources, repeat user attention, and manageable legal/compliance risk.

Very high attention, but also the highest legal, government, platform, and reputational risk.

Examples:

- Will Candidate A win the election?
- Will Party X win more than 100 seats?
- Will a coalition be formed before a specific date?
- Will a named leader resign before year-end?
- Will a bill pass before a specified date?

Why it fits Oro:

- people already debate politics in groups
- yes/no outcomes are easy to understand
- prediction markets are intellectually defensible here
- content pages can rank for election guides, candidate profiles, polling explainers, and outcome trackers

Risks:

- election laws
- government hostility
- misinformation claims
- manipulation concerns
- platform blocking
- market-resolution disputes

Rule:

> Politics should be a later vertical, not the launch wedge. Build credibility first through cricket, football, crypto, entertainment, and creator markets.
